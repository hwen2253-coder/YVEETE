♡ My Reflection in Week 4

✧ Social Media & AI ✧

Our last group project made me reflect on both the limitations of social media research and students' use of AI. We found it difficult to determine whether users were AHC students or University of Leeds students, as their profiles and posts often lacked clear information.

This reminded me to be cautious about drawing conclusions from online identities. Our findings also suggested that many students rely heavily on AI in their everyday lives.

Although AI can be useful and convenient, I worry that excessive reliance could reduce opportunities to develop creativity and independent thinking. This encouraged me to reflect on my own habits and to use AI as a tool to support my ideas while continuing to question, evaluate and think for myself.

This concept connects to our recent group project, where we found it difficult to identify whether social media users were AHC students or University of Leeds students. Their posts did not provide enough evidence to establish their identities.

This made me question how confidently platforms might classify users from similarly incomplete information. For example, searching for AI tools for an assignment could be interpreted as a lasting interest in AI, even when the search serves a temporary purpose.

I therefore think algorithmic identities should be understood as partial and changing interpretations, rather than complete representations of individuals.

♡ Reading Reflection in Week 5

✧ Algorithmic Identity ✧

1. Algorithmic Identity

Algorithmic identity refers to the identity that algorithms construct for us by interpreting data from our online activities, such as searches, clicks and likes.

This identity may differ from how we understand ourselves because it is based on patterns in our data rather than our own explanations of who we are.

2. Social Media Users

He groups his friends into three types: those who mainly share their private lives, those who focus on work and work-related lifestyles, and those who comment on wider social and cultural events.

Although this classification identifies useful patterns, I think it could better account for users who mainly browse without posting, or whose posts combine several topics.
